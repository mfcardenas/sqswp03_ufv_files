**SECTION 3: TERMS AND DEFINITIONS (pages 4-6)**

This is the MOST IMPORTANT section. Read very carefully.

**Critical definitions:**

1. **Usability**
   - Read the complete official definition
   - Identify the 3 components: effectiveness, efficiency, satisfaction
   - Note the key phrase: "**in a specified context of use**"
   
   📝 **Note:** The complete definition textually (you'll need it for the quiz)

2. **Effectiveness**
   - "Accuracy and completeness with which users achieve specified objectives"
   - Ask yourself: Does it refer to WHETHER the objective is achieved or HOW it's achieved?
   
   📝 **Note:** Think of a concrete example of effectiveness metric

3. **Efficiency**
   - "Resources expended in relation to the accuracy and completeness achieved"
   - Resources = time, physical effort, mental effort, financial costs
   - Ask yourself: Why can a system be effective but not efficient?
   
   📝 **Note:** Difference between effectiveness and efficiency with example

4. **Satisfaction**
   - "Extent to which the user's physical, cognitive and emotional responses that result from the use of a system, product or service meet the user's needs and expectations"
   - Includes emotional, cognitive, physical aspects
   - Ask yourself: Can a system be effective and efficient but not satisfactory?
   
   📝 **Note:** Example of unsatisfactory system even though it works

5. **Context of use**
   - "Users, tasks, equipment (hardware, software and materials), and the physical and social environments in which a product is used"
   - This is the foundation of the entire laboratory
   - Identify the 4 components: users, tasks, equipment, environments
   
   📝 **Note:** The 4 components of context of use

**Other relevant definitions:**
- User
- Task
- Product - in our case, TravelEase
- System

**Reading strategy:**
- Don't read continuously, but definition by definition
- After each definition, close your eyes and try to explain it in your own words
- Think of a concrete example before continuing

**SECTION 5: CONTEXT OF USE (pages 8-11)**

This section explains in detail the 4 components of context of use.

**5.1 Users**

Read identifying:
- **User characteristics** (age, gender, skills, experience, physical/cognitive limitations, language, culture)
- **User types** (primary, secondary, indirect, stakeholders)
- **Variability between users** (not all users are the same)

📝 **Note:** 
- What user characteristics are relevant for TravelEase?
- What types of users would TravelEase have?

**5.2 Tasks**

Read identifying:
- **Task objectives** (what does the user want to achieve?)
- **Task structure** (steps, sequence, dependencies)
- **Frequency** (daily, weekly, occasional, unique)
- **Duration** (seconds, minutes, hours)
- **Complexity** (simple, complex, requires decision making)
- **Criticality** (how important is it to complete the task?)

📝 **Note:** 
- Identify 3-5 tasks a user would do in TravelEase
- For each one, determine frequency, duration, complexity

**5.3 Equipment (hardware, software, materials)**

Read identifying:
- **Hardware:** Devices (desktop, laptop, tablet, smartphone, wearable, kiosk)
- **Software:** Operating systems, browsers, apps
- **Technical characteristics:** Screen size, resolution, processing capacity, storage
- **Materials:** Documents, forms, references

📝 **Note:** 
- What devices would TravelEase users use?
- What are the differences between using TravelEase on mobile vs desktop?

**5.4 Environments**

This is one of the richest parts of the standard. Read carefully.

**Physical Environment:**
- Location (home, office, public transport, outdoors)
- Lighting (natural, artificial, variable)
- Noise (quiet, noisy, variable)
- Weather conditions (if applicable)
- Available space
- User posture (sitting, standing, moving)

**Technical Environment:**
- Network infrastructure (WiFi, mobile, speed, reliability)
- Security configurations (firewalls, proxies)
- Integration with other systems

**Social Environment:**
- Individual vs collaborative
- Interruptions and distractions
- Social norms of use
- Presence of other people

**Organizational Environment (if applicable):**
- Organizational structure
- Policies and procedures
- Organizational culture

**Cultural Environment:**
- Language and alphabet
- Cultural conventions (date formats, currency)
- Cultural norms of interaction
- Technological literacy levels

📝 **Note:** 
- For TravelEase, what are the most common physical environments?
- How does the technical environment (e.g., variable WiFi) affect design?
- What cultural aspects are relevant for a tourism platform?

**Reflection questions:**
- Why is it important to analyze context BEFORE designing?
- What would happen if you design TravelEase assuming everyone uses desktop with fast WiFi?
- How would requirements change if the context of use is different?

**SECTION 6: USABILITY MEASURES (pages 11-14)**

This section explains HOW to measure effectiveness, efficiency, and satisfaction.

**6.1 Effectiveness Measures**

The standard proposes metrics such as:
- **Percentage of objectives achieved**
- **Percentage of users who complete tasks successfully**
- **Error rate**
- **Accuracy of results**

📝 **Note:** 
- For the task "book a tourism package in TravelEase", how would you measure effectiveness?
- Example: "95% of users manage to complete a booking without errors"

**6.2 Efficiency Measures**

The standard proposes metrics such as:
- **Time to complete task**
- **Number of actions/steps** (clicks, inputs)
- **Mental effort** (cognitive load)
- **Economic resources**

📝 **Note:** 
- For the same task, how would you measure efficiency?
- Example: "Average time from search to booking: 3 minutes"

**6.3 Satisfaction Measures**

The standard proposes metrics such as:
- **Rating scales** (Likert, 1-5 scale)
- **Standardized questionnaires** (SUS - System Usability Scale, SUMI, QUIS)
- **Net Promoter Score (NPS)**
- **Qualitative data** (comments, interviews)

📝 **Note:** 
- How would you measure satisfaction in TravelEase?
- Research what the System Usability Scale (SUS) is - it's widely used in the industry

**Table 1 of the standard:**
The table summarizes example measures. Study it carefully, it will be useful for defining your metrics.

**Important:**
- Metrics must be **specific to the context of use**
- There are no "universal" metrics - it depends on user, task, environment
- Metrics must be **objectively measurable** (or with clear procedures if subjective)

### Paper Reading Guide

#### Typical Structure of an Academic Paper

1. **Abstract** - Synthesis of the entire paper (1 paragraph)
2. **Introduction** - Context and motivation
3. **Background** - State of the art, related work
4. **Methodology** - How the study was done (if applicable)
5. **Results/Findings** - Main findings
6. **Discussion** - Interpretation of results
7. **Conclusions** - Conclusions and future work
8. **References** - Bibliographic references

#### Reading Strategy (Don't read linearly)

**First step: Quick reading (10 min)**
1. Read the **Abstract** completely
2. Read the **Introduction** and **Conclusions**
3. Look at the **headings** of sections
4. Observe figures and tables

**Objective:** Understand what the paper is about before reading it completely.

**Second step: Deep reading (30 min)**
1. Read section by section
2. Highlight key concepts
3. Note questions or doubts in margins

**Third step: Synthesis (10 min)**
1. Write 3-5 key ideas from the paper
2. Connect with ISO 9241-11 you read before

### What to Look for in the Bevan et al. (2015) Paper

This paper explains the **revision of ISO 9241-11** from 1998 to 2018. It's perfect for understanding the evolution of the concept of usability.

**Guiding questions while reading:**

1. **What changed between ISO 9241-11:1998 and the 2018 version?**
   - Was the definition of usability modified?
   - Were new concepts added?
   
2. **What was learned about usability in 20 years?**
   - How did context of use evolve? (from desktop to mobile, IoT, etc.)
   - What new challenges emerged?

3. **How does usability relate to UX (user experience)?**
   - Are they the same or different?
   - How do they complement each other?

4. **What examples of standard application are mentioned?**
   - In what industries/domains is it applied?
   - What success cases are there?

- 2 concepts that surprised you or you didn't know
